Open canvas

Premium structure editing. Free. Always.

ChemCanvas turns "caffeine" into an editable structure the moment you press Enter. Bonds snap to real geometry, formulas update while you draw, and the whole canvas can be lifted off the page into 3D and held in your hands.

Search

One bar reads names, SMILES, and commands

The search bar checks PubChem for common names and falls back to OPSIN when you hand it strict IUPAC nomenclature. Paste a SMILES string and it validates while you type, showing the formula before you commit. Whatever you searched for lands on the canvas as a normal, editable drawing rather than a flat picture.

Ctrl + K, from anywhere in the app.

Drawing

Drawing that knows the rules

Bonds snap to thirty-degree angles at standard length. Click an atom and the next bond grows into the most open direction, so chains zig-zag the way they do on paper. Drop a benzene ring onto an existing bond and the two fuse into naphthalene. Give a nitrogen one bond too many and a small amber dot appears; hover it and it tells you what went wrong. Nothing blocks you, nothing pops up.

Hold Alt to draw free-angle. G toggles grid snap.

Properties

The numbers are real

Formula, molecular weight, exact mass, SMILES, InChI, InChIKey. All of it comes from RDKit, the open-source chemistry engine used across drug discovery, and all of it updates the moment your drawing changes. Select a single atom and the panel shows its charge, isotope, hybridization, and chirality instead.

Edit the SMILES field directly and the drawing re-renders.

New in 2.3

Put the whole canvas in the room

Press AR and everything you've drawn comes with you — not one structure at a time. ChemCanvas works out each shape, opens your camera, and stands them all up in a row in front of you, spheres for atoms, real depth, real geometry. A progress bar counts the structures as they're built, so you're never staring at an empty screen wondering.

You can see what you're about to grab before you grab it. Whatever your finger and thumb are nearest lights up — a molecule, or a single atom once you're inside one — and the ring changes the moment you actually close the pinch. No guessing, no grabbing the wrong thing.

Pinch any part of a molecule and it grows — right where it stands, no jumping to the middle — while everything else fades back. From then on it's the only thing your hands can reach. Pinch one of its atoms and move your hand to turn it over. Pinch the cross-hair at its centre and it slides instead, following your hand. Pinch with both hands and pull them apart to make it bigger — and while both hands are up it won't let go by accident, so a resize can't drop you out. Pinch clear of it to let it go. Nothing to wear, nothing to set up, no headset — just a camera and your hands.

It's built to be worked in, not just watched: pick any atom to read its element, mass and charge, or measure a bond length or an angle by picking two or three atoms on the same structure.

Ball-and-stick, space-filling, or wireframe · save a photo of what you see · works with a mouse too if you'd rather drag

Reactions, stereochemistry, templates, and a history you can scrub through. It works offline, and it prints in clean black on white.

In the box

What's included in the free software.

AR viewer

See any structure in 3D over your camera and move it with your hands. Ball-and-stick, space-filling, or wireframe, with a photo button for slides and posters.

Reaction arrows

Forward, equilibrium, retrosynthetic, resonance, and curly electron-pushing arrows, with conditions text above and below. One command lines up reactants, arrow, and products.

Stereochemistry

Wedge, dash, and wavy bonds. R/S and E/Z labels appear on their own and follow every edit. A Newman projection is one right-click away.

Templates

Rings, functional groups, all twenty amino acids, nucleotide bases, and common drug scaffolds. Save your own fragments and reuse them later.

History

Every step is kept. A panel lists them all, and clicking an entry jumps the document straight back to that point.

Files

Opens MOL, SDF, SMILES, and RXN. Exports the same, plus PNG up to 600 dpi, SVG, and PDF for anything headed to print.

Stays local

Drawings autosave to your machine and reopen where you left off. The only network requests are name lookups, and only when you ask for one.

Privacy

Privacy policy

The short version: nothing about you is collected — no accounts, no telemetry, no analytics, no cookies, no tracking. Your drawings live on your computer. Two features do send a structure or a name out to a public chemistry service when you use them, and both are listed below.

This website

  • Sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser — not even a flag remembering that you accepted this policy, which is why the download prompt may ask again.
  • Runs no analytics, no tracking scripts, and no third-party embeds. Fonts and images are served from this site itself, so loading the page contacts no one but the host that serves it.
  • Has no accounts, no forms, and no comment system. There is nothing here that could transmit what you type, because you cannot type anything.
  • Links to external sites (LinkedIn, RDKit, PubChem, OPSIN) load nothing until you click them.

The software

  • Runs entirely on your computer. Drawings autosave to your own machine and reopen from there. Nothing is uploaded, synced, or backed up anywhere.
  • Contains no telemetry, no crash reporting, no usage statistics, no update pinging, and no account system.
  • Name lookup. When you type a chemical name into search, or ask ChemCanvas to name a structure you drew, that text or structure is sent over HTTPS to PubChem (U.S. National Institutes of Health) or OPSIN (University of Cambridge) to resolve it.
  • Precise 3D in the AR viewer. When you open AR, every structure on your canvas — not just one you picked — is sent as a SMILES string to NCI CACTUS (U.S. National Cancer Institute) to fetch properly embedded 3D coordinates, which are markedly more accurate than the shape ChemCanvas can derive on its own. The AR viewer works without this — it renders locally-built shapes first, and precise ones only replace them as they arrive. You can turn it off with the Precise 3D button inside the AR viewer, and the viewer tells you which geometry you're looking at.
  • Both of the above are only triggered by an action you take. Like any web request, those services can see your IP address, and their own policies govern what they do with what they receive. If you don't use name lookup and turn off precise 3D, the app makes no network requests at all and works fully offline.
  • If you work on unpublished structures, note that using those features means the structure reaches a third party. That's the reason each one is optional and labeled rather than silent.
  • Discards any cookies those lookup services attempt to set. Nothing persists between requests.
  • The AR viewer asks for your camera only when you open it, and stops the camera the moment you leave. Frames are read for hand tracking and discarded immediately — never recorded, never uploaded. The hand-tracking model ships inside the app rather than loading from anyone's server, so using AR contacts no one at all.

Before you download

ChemCanvas collects no data. In plain terms:

  • Everything runs and saves locally on your computer.
  • No telemetry, no account, no cookies.
  • Name lookups you trigger send only the typed text to PubChem or OPSIN; skip the search bar and it never touches the network.
  • Precise 3D, if you turn it on in AR, sends your structures to NCI CACTUS for exact coordinates. Off by choice at any time.

The full text is in the privacy policy. Downloading means you agree to it.
